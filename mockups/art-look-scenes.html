Storybook Parchment

Premium read: It reads as a genuinely charming, cohesive children's-book village — warm and inviting with real craft in the wavy eaves and glowing windows — though the flat SVG gradients keep it one notch below a truly hand-painted premium illustration.

Pros

  • Most native to the Warm Parchment palette you picked — style + color reinforce each other
  • Warmest, coziest, most "gains-only" tone
  • Coheres with your approved parchment world map; soft warmth forgives asset mismatches

Cons

  • True painterly quality isn't free CC0 — last 10% needs a commission or AI-to-style
  • Softer / less "game-y" and crisp than the others
  • Hardest to source at scale purely from free art
How it's made: True 2:1 isometric massing (every wall edge runs on the same ±26.57-degree axis), then dressed in storybook warmth. The hand-drawn line quality comes from a single thick warm-brown stroke (#7a6442) on every form with rounded line-joins, plus deliberately wavy roof eaves drawn with quadratic Bezier curves instead of straight lines so the thatch overhangs irregularly like a painted picture book. Depth is read three ways: soft brown ground-shadow ellipses under each structure, a back-to-front paint order (tower/chapel first, cottage hero last so it overlaps), and a one-step value shift between the lit left wall (cream gradient) and shaded right wall (tan gradient). Roofs use a honey-to-deep-gold vertical gradient with a translucent #c98a1e panel on the right slope for cozy modeling. Warmth and cohesion come from sitting everything on a rounded radial-gradient parchment patch under a soft sun glow with puffy outlined clouds and curl-of-smoke chimneys, glowing honey windows, and emerald foliage with deep-emerald shaded undersides. Gains-only: no dark sky, no grim tones, every accent is warm.  ·  Grade does ~80–90%; commission/AI-to-style for hero polish (Phase 2)

Premium read: It reads as a genuinely charming, cohesive cozy-game tile — warm and inviting with real craft, though the deliberately chunky pixel aesthetic feels more "indie-handmade delightful" than slick high-end polish.

Pros

  • Largest clean free CC0 coverage — a whole town, free, today
  • Fastest to build; no render tooling; trivially recolors to the palette
  • Cozy, nostalgic, charming

Cons

  • Reads "retro sim-game," clashes with your premium Clash-Display/Satoshi brand
  • Low-res (16px / 128×64) looks dated next to a modern UI; blocky scaling
  • All-or-nothing aesthetic; premium ceiling is low
How it's made: Every form is built only from axis-aligned <rect> blocks snapped to a coarse ~4px grid, with shape-rendering="crispEdges" on every group so edges stay hard and aliasing-free — the signature 16-bit look. Volume comes from a strict 2-3 tone scheme per surface (lit cream #f4ecd8 left faces, shaded tan #e7d8b0 right faces, a single warm-brown #7a6442 seam as the "outline"), never gradients. Iso depth is faked by stair-stepping rects: roofs narrow upward in fixed 8-12px steps, crenellations and paths step diagonally, and chimney smoke rises as offset single pixels. The scene depth-sorts by drawing a far row (keep, chapel, little house, back tree) first, then a near row (cottage, market stall, gate wall, front trees) on top so nearer objects overlap farther ones. Accents (honey/deep-gold roofs and banners, emerald/deep-emerald foliage clumps) stay flat-filled and limited to the seven-color palette.  ·  Recolor CC0 pixel packs (Screaming Brain 443 tiles, Kenney Tiny Town) — drops in as-is

Premium read: It reads as the most expensive of the three: the gradient-shaded faces, rim light, soft cast shadows and AO genuinely evoke a Blender-rendered isometric sprite, though up close it's still clean vector geometry rather than true painterly texture.

Pros

  • Highest premium ceiling reachable with free art ("polished city-builder")
  • Stays CC0 + zero-dep; warm-parchment lighting baked into the render = cohesive
  • Crisp & modern; sits well next to a premium UI

Cons

  • Needs a one-time Blender pipeline (I script it; you judge renders)
  • Heaviest assets → more build/optimization work
  • Stylized not hand-painted; slowest to first art
How it's made: Every face carries a multi-stop linear gradient running light-to-dark top-to-bottom (e.g. #fbf5e6 to #e9dcbe on lit walls, #f4d77a to #c98a1e on shaded roofs), so each plane reads as a lit 3D surface rather than flat fill. Top edges get a thin warm rim-light stroke (#fff3cf at low opacity) to mimic a key light grazing the geometry. Crevices and eaves carry semi-transparent warm-brown (rgba(122,100,66,0.4)) ambient-occlusion slivers. Each object floats on a blurred radial soft-shadow ellipse on the ground for a grounded pre-render feel. True 2:1 isometric projection throughout, depth-sorted far-to-near so the front cottage and tree overlap the keep and chapel. All gradient/filter ids are prefixed (p25-, p25c-, p25t-, p25h-) to stay collision-free on one page.  ·  Route 2 — pre-render CC0 3D (KayKit) in Blender with warm-parchment lighting

How to choose

  • Storybook is the most native to the palette you picked and the coziest — but peak quality needs a paid hero-art step later.
  • Painterly 2.5D gets you closest to premium for free (KayKit + Blender), and can render into this exact warm lighting — but needs a build pipeline.
  • Pixel is the fast/cheap route; charming but reads retro, not premium.
  • My pick for your brand + palette: Painterly 2.5D as the build target, Storybook as the tone to grade toward — they're close cousins. Tell me which you react to.

Reminder: whichever you pick, the build never relies on color alone (emerald+gold is colorblind-tricky) — towns/states also get labels/shapes, with a blue "current" highlight.
